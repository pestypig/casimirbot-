# CFP-1 hosted pilot price and capacity assay v1

## Working candidate, not a customer term

**Current owner-directed review candidate:** the later [provisional hosted-term selection](eh-g8-cfp1-provisional-hosted-term-selection-v1.md) chooses `$20/month`, 50 verified paid effects and the existing ten-effect trial for D07 validation. The `$10` and `$5` cases below remain historical sensitivity comparators. The later selection does not cure their missing B/P/T, account, support, refund, tax or rights evidence and is not approved public copy.

Use the **USD $20-per-month sponsoring-host subscription** as the primary D07 cost assay, with applicable tax shown separately only if the qualified tax review supports that presentation. The [authorized Stripe read](../evidence/eh-g8-codex-first-paid-product-delivery-v1/cfp-1/2026-09-20-cfp1-stripe-authorized-dashboard-read-121.md) found a $10 monthly *sandbox credit* Product, but it **cannot be reused as the hosted SKU** merely because its interval resembles the proposed subscription. The $10 case and hypothetical $5 monthly case remain historical sensitivity comparators; the visible $5 sandbox Product is a one-time credit purchase, not either subscription.

Measure this proposed benefit under **one sponsor, one active room, one invited authenticated guest and one connected program**, using the [provisional first-party domain-browser guest request path](eh-g8-cfp1-hosted-guest-request-ingress-v1.md) as the P/T baseline. Include distinct host/guest account sessions, invitation, owner pre-grant, guest request, native result inspection and return visits without a guest GPT/Codex account or CasimirBot-funded model call. A separately supported guest MCP variant may be measured in addition if selected for first-customer copy. The primary paid target is **50 verified successful bounded shared effects** in a monthly term; use 10 and 100 as lower/adverse workload sensitivity points. The [reduced-hour screen](../evidence/eh-g8-codex-first-paid-product-delivery-v1/cfp-1/2026-09-20-cfp1-reduced-room-hour-assay-134.md) makes **20 active paid room-hours** the next measurement envelope, with the earlier 40-hour case retained as adverse stress. These are load-sampling points, not a proposed customer time allowance or an entitlement. A 100-effect customer ceiling is not selected. The primary economic admission candidate is a per-sponsor internal PBT allowance, paired with finite room leases and hard request, compute, egress and storage ceilings so idle presence and sustained work cannot run indefinitely. The current source enforces none of these commercial limits.

The [owner-selected provisional trial](../evidence/eh-g8-codex-first-paid-product-delivery-v1/cfp-1/2026-09-19-provisional-hosted-trial-limits-owner-selection-48.md) remains a continuous seven-day, no-card term with one room, one guest, one program and ten verified effects. Use **two active trial room-hours** as the next measurement point, not a selected trial limit; retain the earlier seven-hour point as adverse stress. Test both a sponsor who uses the whole envelope and a sponsor who leaves the room open with little action. Verify that two active hours still let a guest experience the ten-effect benefit across the seven-day trial; otherwise revise the workload and price. No automatic paid conversion, credit posting or model/API resale enters either case. The action must first pass connector, owner-grant, native-result and commercial-rights review; Minecraft is conditional, with the separately proposed non-game fallback if it does not qualify.

The [open-browser room source audit](../evidence/eh-g8-codex-first-paid-product-delivery-v1/cfp-1/2026-09-20-cfp1-open-browser-room-cost-source-audit-212.md) makes the unattended trial a separate cost case. Today's experimental room continues database-backed refresh and presence work when its dialog closes; that behavior is not evidence of the proposed first-party no-model guest page's eventual cadence. For the selected page, measure or source-bound a host and guest leaving the browser open through the seven-day calendar term, including database active-time union and idle tails, requests, compute, egress, storage and return visits. The 10/1 and 20/2 active-hour samples cannot bound this case. Before using PBT to freeze the offer, establish a finite idle/lease behavior with proven suspension and authority-checked return, or carry an enforceable worst-case sustained-open bound through the low-paying/high-trial cohort. The seven-day trial clock and ten-effect target remain separate from the internal resource budget.

Use [finite, explicitly renewed room activity](eh-g8-cfp1-hosted-guest-request-ingress-v1.md) as the **first assay design**: a background tab or heartbeat cannot keep the room's active lease alive by itself. Record the candidate idle threshold and lease length as test inputs, not customer limits; test that routine database-backed work stops after suspension and that an intentional return reserves capacity before resuming. Include ten effects spread across separate trial visits so a low passive-cost design is not achieved by making the trial unusable. A continuous-open cost case remains an adverse comparison and a fallback only if a separately reviewed, enforceable budget covers it.

Use the [D07 finite-lease sensitivity](eh-g8-cfp1-d07-provider-unit-allocation-and-assay-v1.md) to compare ten effect-producing visits, thirty visits with only ten effects, and intentionally sustained use at five- and fifteen-minute candidate lease lengths. The provider's five-minute idle tail makes the isolated no-background database-time ceilings 100/200 minutes for ten separated visits and 300/600 for thirty; real incremental cost requires the observed request union, B overlap and every other meter. These figures are neither selected time allowances nor PBT capacities. Repeated returns can exhaust a cost-based trial budget even when the effect count remains below ten; prove visible suspension, safe admission denial and owner stop without silently shortening the seven-day calendar term.

The sponsor term owns the counters. Handoff to another eligible host begins a new sponsor interval; it never transfers paid PBT, trial days, effect quota or another person's program grant. Count only a native-postcondition-verified successful effect against the effect ceiling; hold an uncertain result separately until reconciliation. Measure active room-hours from durable open/pause/close intervals, excluding a fully suspended room but including a connected room even when no effect occurs; use them to attribute cost and renew finite leases, not as the default customer allowance. CFP-3 must test concurrent requests, retries, reconnect, room recreation, sponsor switch and period reset before these can become customer terms.

The [current source audit](../evidence/eh-g8-codex-first-paid-product-delivery-v1/cfp-1/2026-09-20-cfp1-hosted-cap-source-boundary-142.md) found no sponsor-term room-hour or verified-effect quota in the room, sandbox billing or action broker paths. The existing two-person room capacity and per-action authority effect budget do **not** enforce these commercial ceilings. Any price case using 20/2 hours or 100/10 effects is conditional on specified CFP-3 admission and durable accounting; it must also bound request/compute/egress/storage/support dimensions that those two counters do not cap.

## Price headroom, not a margin finding

For a **hypothetical** U.S. domestic-card monthly charge under [Stripe's published standard Payments rate](https://stripe.com/pricing) of 2.9% plus $0.30 and [published Billing pay-as-you-go rate](https://stripe.com/billing/pricing) of 0.7% of Billing volume, the primary `$20` candidate leaves **$18.98** after those two modeled fees. The `$10` and `$5` comparators leave **$9.34** and **$4.52**. A later [account-context sandbox Plans read](../evidence/eh-g8-codex-first-paid-product-delivery-v1/cfp-1/2026-09-20-cfp1-stripe-sandbox-plan-fee-source-246.md) displayed those rate shapes in test mode, so they are dated sandbox plan assumptions rather than public comparators alone. The sandbox Fees tab still displayed no deducted fees. This is simple arithmetic before per-component rounding, refunds, disputes, tax handling, hosting, identity, database, delivery, support and trial costs; the effective live rate and hosted Price remain unverified.

For a review cohort with **10 paying hosts and 30 trial starts** in the same monthly accounting interval, let `F` be allocated fixed hosted cost, `P` the marginal monthly cost per paying host, `T` the marginal cost per seven-day trial start, and `R` all refunds, disputes, extra payment costs and nonrecoverable tax not already counted. At the primary `$20` candidate, break-even requires:

```text
F + 10P + 30T + R < 10 × $18.98 = $189.80
```

For a 20% **gross-revenue contribution sensitivity**, not a selected margin promise, the same total must be **at most $149.80**. At the historical `$10` comparator, break-even and 20% ceilings are **$93.40** and **$73.40**; at `$5` they are **$45.20** and **$35.20**. These inequalities make the impact of trial conversion and shared hosting explicit; they do not prove any term viable. Re-run with lower paid-host and higher trial-start counts, international-card/FX fee cases if those markets are selected, actual refund policy and the final room/effect envelope.

The [small-cohort signing-cost sensitivity](../evidence/eh-g8-codex-first-paid-product-delivery-v1/cfp-1/2026-09-20-cfp1-small-cohort-signing-cost-sensitivity-261.md) inserts one **illustrative** `$9.99` monthly individual-signing cost into the same fee case. One `$20` sponsor would leave **$8.99 before any other cost**; ten would leave **$179.81** for every other fixed, paid and trial cost. The 20%-of-gross sensitivity for ten sponsors leaves **$139.81** after signing for those other costs. The historical `$10` case remains materially narrower. This is whole-product arithmetic with a dated approximate signing price, not the owner's actual signing bill or a finding that `$20` is viable. Allocate the real signing line across free personal and hosted cohorts once, reconcile to the product total, and repeat the low-paying/high-trial case before final D07 selection.

The [conditional Auth0 step-up cost screen](../evidence/eh-g8-codex-first-paid-product-delivery-v1/cfp-1/2026-09-20-cfp1-auth0-step-up-small-cohort-cost-screen-264.md) adds a **possible** `$35/month` Essentials/Pro MFA fixed line to the same signing and Stripe-fee scenario. If that tier is required, five `$20` sponsors leave **$49.91** after the illustrative `$9.99 + $35` fixed lines and before hosting, free-personal use, trial starts or support; ten leave **$144.81**, or **$104.81** after the 20%-of-gross contribution holdback. This is not the tenant's verified future plan or a requirement to buy MFA. Price the actual accepted identity/factor and recovery route in D07; do not silently enter zero for a paid plan or assume a free passkey flow meets D02's operation-time challenge.

The [database active-time stress](../evidence/eh-g8-codex-first-paid-product-delivery-v1/cfp-1/2026-09-20-cfp1-database-active-time-stress-124.md) applies the account's privately recorded rate to the original 40/7 paid/trial hour points. In a feasible non-overlap case, database compute alone exceeds the modeled $10 cohort receipts after the two public comparator fees. The [reduced 20/2 screen](../evidence/eh-g8-codex-first-paid-product-delivery-v1/cfp-1/2026-09-20-cfp1-reduced-room-hour-assay-134.md) passes a database-only 20% contribution sensitivity, but **does not** cover the other costs or establish actual marginal load. Shared activity and an already-active database could lower incremental active time; idle tails could raise it. Measure active minutes, overlap and every remaining cost input before selecting D07. Keep the private rate and derived account figures outside the public packet.

The [Autoscale saturation stress](../evidence/eh-g8-codex-first-paid-product-delivery-v1/cfp-1/2026-09-20-cfp1-autoscale-saturation-cost-stress-146.md) tests the same 20/2-hour envelope at the observed instance size under a deliberately adverse full-utilization assumption. Compute plus database leave only narrow modeled headroom at $10 before other costs and fail the 20%-of-gross sensitivity; this is not measured room utilization or a complete upper bound. The next B/P/T run must record billable compute units during and outside room intervals, including burst-then-idle and sustained-use cases. A conditional price cannot rely on room-hour/effect ceilings unless it also specifies finite limits or sourced upper bounds for requests, CPU/memory work, egress, storage and support.

The [room-hour feasibility comparison](../evidence/eh-g8-codex-first-paid-product-delivery-v1/cfp-1/2026-09-20-cfp1-room-hour-feasibility-comparison-152.md) adds **ten paid / one trial active room-hour** as a cost-containment *measurement comparator* alongside 20/2. At the illustrative $10 monthly fee case it survives the deliberately adverse compute-plus-database screen with partial headroom, while 20/2 fails the 20%-of-gross sensitivity on those resources alone. Neither point has all-in costs, enforced sponsor limits or demonstrated trial usefulness. Do not shrink the seven-day term or ten-effect trial target from this arithmetic; test whether ten guest effects can be usefully spread across one active hour before any final limit choice.

The [owner's resource-token direction](../evidence/eh-g8-codex-first-paid-product-delivery-v1/cfp-1/2026-09-20-cfp1-owner-resource-token-direction-154.md) changes the **proposed costing method**, not the recorded workload points: use 20/2 and 10/1 hours to measure resource use, then evaluate a versioned internal [PBT budget](eh-g8-cfp1-hosted-resource-budget-token-contract-v1.md) as the primary economic admission limit. Do not turn either sampled hour count into a customer allowance by default. Calendar trial duration, finite native-action deadlines, one-room/guest/program scope and the ten verified trial effects remain separate constraints. A PBT cap must be tested against burst, idle and sustained use and paired with hard request/compute/egress/storage bounds; neither a token nor a subscription buys another owner's action grant.
