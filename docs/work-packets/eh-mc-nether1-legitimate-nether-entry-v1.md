## Timing and purpose

This is the first reserved Minecraft integration objective after G7 closed.
G8 now permits the N0 deterministic capability-readiness course and the staged
direct/keyed acceptance ladder defined below. This packet does not replace the
work program, broaden mutation authority, or permit later acceptance stages to
skip their required evidence. It preserves the accepted G1-G7 dependency chain
and gives later development and Runtime Codex sessions one durable objective to
recover after individual failures.

The demonstration objective is:

> Prepare for the Nether, build and ignite a portal legitimately in survival,
> enter it, establish a safe arrival and return point, and report the verified
> resulting state.

The impressive result is not one lucky scripted run. It is a visible proof
that Codex can interpret an objective, acquire current evidence, use ordinary
Minecraft mechanics, maintain viability, correct failed assumptions, preserve
durable progress, and complete through the same governed lifecycle exposed by
CasimirBot and authenticated MCP.

## Authentic-play contract

The accepted ruleset is `survival_tas` through the selected player's Player
Embodiment. Success may use legal movement, looking, attack/use input,
inventory interaction, crafting, mining, placement, container interaction, and
other explicitly admitted survival workflows. Fabric may execute admitted
finite programs and resident safety responses at tick cadence.

World Authority is a separate capability plane and is not admitted for this
objective. In particular, the acceptance run may not use commands or equivalent
effects to grant items, teleport, change game mode, place the completed portal,
ignite it remotely, modify advancements, suppress ordinary hazards, or declare
the objective complete. Read-only diagnostics may support development, but a
diagnostic or direct-console result is not end-to-end acceptance.

The user grants the initial player/environment authority and retains manual
override and Emergency Stop. After the accepted run begins, ordinary goal
progress must not depend on the user performing gameplay steps for Codex.

## Durable objective and milestone semantics

Helix persists the user's objective, exact environment identity, constraints,
verified progress, unresolved postconditions, attempt history, and recovery
state. Runtime Codex authors the actual milestone plan from current evidence.
The adapter may expose mechanics and capability facts but must not prescribe a
fixed portal walkthrough or silently select strategy.

An illustrative plan may contain:

```text
inspect current player, inventory, terrain, hazards, and available mechanics
→ establish immediate survival viability and a recoverable home/reference point
→ acquire and craft the resources Codex determines are necessary
→ locate or create a legitimate portal-building opportunity
→ construct and verify a valid frame
→ ignite and verify an active portal
→ prepare a bounded Nether-entry safety repertoire
→ enter and stabilize the arrival area
→ verify a usable return portal and record both sides' coordinates/state
→ propose completion from current evidence
```

These are required outcome families, not a deterministic action order. Codex
may mine obsidian, cast a frame with water and lava, change resource strategy,
choose different tools, or pause when the environment makes a safe path
temporarily unavailable. A checkpoint advances only from measured postconditions.

## Correction and self-repair anchor

Every Runtime Codex continuation reconstructs this bounded anchor before
choosing the next action:

```text
objective and authentic-play constraints
→ latest verified checkpoint
→ current viable player/world observation
→ incomplete milestone and required postconditions
→ failed attempts and newly learned constraints
→ current authority, resource, and reaction budgets
→ next bounded experiment or accurate blocker
```

A failure never erases the larger objective and never becomes a reason to
repeat an unchanged physical mutation blindly. Failed attempts remain immutable
provenance. Runtime recovery follows this loop:

1. stop, release, or stabilize locally when continuing would reduce viability;
2. measure the resulting player, inventory, world, target, and control state;
3. classify the first failed assumption or postcondition;
4. re-enter the typed evidence into Runtime Codex;
5. preserve completed checkpoints and revise only the unresolved strategy;
6. choose a materially informed bounded retry, alternate method, pause, or
   actionable failure;
7. verify the new postcondition before advancing the durable milestone.

The response depends on the failure layer:

| First failure layer | Correct owner and response |
| --- | --- |
| Minecraft mechanics or planning assumption | Runtime Codex uses fresh evidence and mechanics retrieval to replan. |
| Fabric action, sensor, pathing, crafting, targeting, or resident-control limitation | The workflow returns an accurate typed limitation; Development Codex repairs or expands the general capability before the unchanged objective is retried. |
| Helix admission, re-entry, continuation, materialization, or presentation contradiction after direct success | Development Codex performs a first-divergence parity audit and repairs the shared adapter contract, never the prompt-specific walkthrough. |
| Identity, consent, stale source, expired lease, manual override, or hard evidence boundary | Helix fails closed with the exact actionable reason; the goal remains suspended rather than falsely completed. |
| External resource or unsupported-world blocker | Runtime Codex records the blocker and safe state, then pauses or selects an admitted alternative if one exists. |

Repair budgets must be finite per action and turn. Exhaustion produces an
accurate checkpointed blocker, not a soft lock or a fabricated success. A later
turn may resume from a fresh observation without creating a replacement goal.

## Fast and slow loops

The demonstration requires two cooperating timescales rather than one model
call per keypress:

```text
Runtime Codex semantic loop
observe → choose milestone → author bounded program → interpret outcome → replan

Fabric resident/execution loop
sense → arbitrate → move/look/interact → protect viability → verify → release
```

Fabric may continuously maintain only the admitted finite objective and safety
repertoire. It cannot invent the next semantic milestone. Meaningful deviation,
repertoire exhaustion, checkpoint completion, or changed goal viability becomes
a compact G4-style wake for Codex. Repeated unchanged ticks do not.

## Capability-readiness increment

Before the first controlled live attempt, inventory the current provider-visible
Minecraft capability catalog against these families:

- current player, inventory, equipment, recipe/craftability, terrain, hazard,
  portal, dimension, and target observations;
- general navigation and stuck detection;
- block/entity targeting and legitimate interaction reach;
- mining, collection, crafting, smelting/furnace, inventory/container transfer,
  equipment, block placement, ignition, and portal entry;
- resident air, health, food, fire/lava, fall, collision, manual-override, and
  control-release protection;
- postcondition measurement for every state-changing operation; and
- durable checkpoints, wake re-entry, retry/cancel/status, and terminal parity.

Missing capability breadth must be added as general Minecraft operations with
adversarial and continuation tests. Do not add a monolithic `build_nether_portal`
answering function or grow retired `server/routes/agi.plan.ts`. Mechanics
retrieval should teach Runtime Codex what is possible; it must not become a
second hidden planner.

## Staged acceptance ladder

### N0 — Deterministic capability course

Use fixtures and a controlled survival world to prove the required observation,
crafting, inventory, navigation, construction, portal, guardian, checkpoint,
and recovery primitives independently. This is deterministic verification, not
goal acceptance.

### N1 — Controlled-world direct Codex

Provide an ordinary survival test area with discoverable wood, stone, iron,
water, lava, and room to build. Reference Codex receives consented direct access
to the same public Fabric capabilities and completes the unchanged natural
objective. Record only public calls, observations, retries, postconditions, and
synthesis. Do not record hidden reasoning.

### N2 — Controlled-world keyed Helix parity

Restore equivalent starting state and submit the unchanged objective through
keyed Helix. Compare direct and governed traces at request, admission, execution,
normalization, exact observation re-entry, follow-up reasoning, route product,
terminal writer, and visible/API/applicable voice presentation. Stop at the
first divergence. Direct success is diagnostic evidence, not permission to
bypass Helix.

### N3 — Known ordinary survival seed

Run without a prepared resource course on a known seed. Codex may inspect the
world and retrieve mechanics but receives no hardcoded coordinate walkthrough.
Accept representative resource-search, night/hazard, blocked-route, tool or
inventory-pressure, and strategy-revision cases.

### N4 — Unknown-world recorded demonstration

Start from a fresh ordinary survival world whose useful resource layout is not
encoded in the adapter or prompt. From user consent onward, Codex and the
harness complete the objective without user gameplay intervention. CasimirBot
and Codex project the same durable run, public actions, evidence, checkpoints,
current blocker/replan state, and supported completion. The recording may show
these public surfaces but never secrets or hidden reasoning.

## Required completion postconditions

Completion requires current evidence proving all of the following:

1. the selected player remained in the admitted survival ruleset;
2. required resources were acquired and transformed through admitted survival
   interactions with verified inventory/world deltas;
3. a valid portal was constructed and active without World Authority substitution;
4. the selected player entered the Nether through that portal;
5. the arrival state was viable and all automated controls were released or
   remained inside an explicitly active bounded program;
6. a usable return portal was observed in the Nether and both sides' relevant
   dimension/position evidence was checkpointed;
7. representative deviations caused measured correction, safe suspension, or
   accurate typed failure rather than goal abandonment or false completion;
8. the durable goal ledger covers every required milestone and retains failed
   attempts;
9. the final Runtime Codex candidate cites the required current observations;
10. Helix terminal authority selects that candidate without downstream
    substitution; and
11. Codex MCP, CasimirBot UI/API, and applicable room/voice presentation agree
    on the same supported terminal product and certainty.

## Stop and fail criteria

Stop the run safely and preserve a resumable checkpoint when identity, consent,
source/world/player binding, authority, observation freshness, resident health,
or execution lease cannot be verified. Emergency Stop or manual override always
wins and releases controls.

Do not claim acceptance when:

- a human completed an undisclosed gameplay step after the accepted run began;
- a server command, creative effect, teleport, item grant, or prebuilt active
  portal satisfied an authentic survival postcondition;
- the adapter encoded the demonstrated route or portal plan;
- success was inferred from a receipt without measured world and player state;
- the direct route passed but keyed Helix dropped or replaced the supported
  Codex result;
- a crash, death, disconnect, or exhausted retry silently created a new goal;
- credentials, hidden reasoning, or unrestricted host access entered evidence;
  or
- resource pressure made the run or its verification artifacts unreliable.

## Evidence and recording package

Each accepted stage retains:

- world/seed fixture identity appropriate to the stage, mod/plugin and
  capability-manifest hashes, selected player, source/world/epoch, and leases;
- natural objective, durable goal ID/revision, milestone/checkpoint chain, and
  attempt history;
- admitted calls, bounded programs, ordered observations, postconditions,
  semantic wakes, replans, cancellations, and resident interventions;
- inventory and world deltas sufficient to prove authentic progress without
  exporting raw credentials or unrelated private state;
- candidate, route-product, terminal-writer, visible/API and applicable voice
  hashes; and
- latency, tick continuity, memory pressure, crash/restart, manual override,
  Emergency Stop, and final control-release status.

The public demonstration overlay should favor the current objective, verified
checkpoint, active bounded program, guardian state, meaningful deviation,
next Codex decision, and supported completion. It must not present classifier
guesses, hidden chain of thought, raw tokens, or debug projections as answer
authority.
