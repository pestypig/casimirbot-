# Thermal population and accelerator-upscattering lead

Program gate: S1. Conditional energy-budget screen; no source attribution or joint rate.

## Primary lead

[McKeen et al., 2202.08840v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2202.08840v2) study accumulated Earth-bound dark matter and its deliberate upscattering by underground accelerator beams, followed by detection downstream. They discuss a hidden fermion with a dark-photon interaction and distinguish equilibrated and transient sinking populations. Their introduction includes CASPAR among underground accelerators. This is motivation to inspect a model and source configuration; it does not establish accelerator operation, alignment, transport or an association with the LZ candidate. Historical facility specifications in a theory paper are not contemporaneous operating records.

## Derived thermal obstruction

For nonrelativistic elastic scattering on a stationary nucleus, the incident kinetic energy needed for recoil E_R is E_min=E_R(m_chi+m_A)^2/(4 m_chi m_A), which is at least E_R. A Maxwell distribution therefore carries a Boltzmann exponential no larger than exp(-E_R/kT) at threshold, apart from polynomial rate factors. At 248 keV the minimum exponent is 9.59307e6 for 300 K and 575584 for 5000 K. These are declared temperatures, not measured local dark-matter temperatures. No exact zero-tail statement or density-normalized rate is inferred.

A thermalized terrestrial bath alone cannot plausibly provide this hard elastic recoil through an ordinary thermal tail. A shared-species model needs a surviving fast component, a quantified acceleration source or an energy-releasing channel. Its slow density cannot be freely fitted: capture, evaporation, sinking, annihilation where present, and chamber access must be calculated from the interaction and incident abundance.

## Two-step endpoint calculation

For a projectile of mass m_b and kinetic energy E_b elastically scattering on initially stationary dark matter, followed by elastic scattering of that dark matter on stationary xenon, the maximum recoil is

E_R,max = E_b [4 m_b m_chi/(m_b+m_chi)^2] [4 m_chi m_Xe/(m_chi+m_Xe)^2].

Both endpoints require favorable scattering angles. Optimizing over m_chi gives m_chi=sqrt(m_b m_Xe). With representative m_Xe=122 GeV:

| Assumed projectile energy | Optimal DM mass | Maximum Xe recoil | Minimum projectile energy for 248 keV |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1.1 MeV proton | 10.699 GeV | 96.705 keV | 2.82094 MeV |
| 2.2 MeV alpha | 21.325 GeV | 564.602 keV | 0.966345 MeV |

The energies are illustrative inputs, not authenticated beam settings. In particular a terminal voltage does not specify alpha kinetic energy without charge state and acceleration configuration. Kinematic accessibility supplies no rate: angular acceptance, source distance, intervening rock, particle flux, operation timing and the same dark-matter coupling remain essential.

## Decision and next evidence

Accelerated captured dark matter is a concrete alternative to fitting two unrelated incident populations. It may allow the slow part to affect local coherence while a small accelerated part creates hard xenon recoils. No overlap has been demonstrated. Before any event-origin suggestion, authenticate source history and geometry, read the explicit portal and accumulated-density model, and calculate production plus attenuation and detector acceptance. A failure of timing or geometry would reject that source hypothesis without modifying the physics to fit the event.

This model is separate from previous halo-only and compact-composite pilots. Do not reuse their full density for an added captured component or their photon-free baryon response for a dark photon without atomic screening and charge matching. The frozen coherence apparatus remains unchanged.
