# Gamma-ray claim cross-check for the bosonic compatibility screen

Date: September 25, 2026. Status: exploratory literature audit; no dark-matter detection claim is adopted.

## Purpose

The three papers supplied in this workstream concern different observables and hypotheses. Their results must not be combined as if they were independent confirmations of one particle. This screen asks whether any of them currently narrows the shared boson-star / LZ / Casimir-DP candidate enough to justify a specific prediction model.

## What the papers establish

* [Fan et al., arXiv:2407.11737 (revised July 21, 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11737) report a candidate 43.2 GeV gamma-ray line in a Fermi-LAT stack of Virgo, Fornax and Ophiuchus. Their quoted net test statistic is about 30 for those three clusters and about 21 when ten other clusters are included. The line is absent in the inner Galaxy in their analysis; the authors state this disfavors the canonical annihilation interpretation and call for a more sophisticated DM model or unusual astrophysics. This is a candidate line feature, not a particle identification or an LZ-compatible interaction measurement. A monochromatic 43.2 GeV photon also differs spectrally from the continuum gamma rays expected from the LZ IDM point's listed electroweak final states.
* [Totani, arXiv:2507.07209 (v3, November 23, 2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07209) reports a 20 GeV-peaked, high-latitude Milky Way halo-like excess and gives a conditional DM fit of roughly 0.5–0.8 TeV annihilating to `b bbar` at `(5–8)e-25 cm^3/s`. The paper itself notes tension with dwarf-galaxy bounds and the canonical thermal rate, and emphasizes halo-profile uncertainty. This is not the 1.08 TeV IDM best-fit from LZ; the mass and final state differ.
* [Stenhouse, Ghag and Deppisch, arXiv:2607.08552 (July 9, 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08552) independently reproduce the halo-like feature with cellwise and pixel-level analyses. Their conditional spectral fits are about 0.55 TeV to `W+W-` or 0.72 TeV to `b bbar`, with rate around `1e-24 cm^3/s`. They report 4–5-fold dwarf-spheroidal tension under their baseline comparison, broadened to 1.6–9.3 after foreground and J-factor systematics; their abstract identifies a finely velocity-structured low-velocity enhancement as the viable route to reconcile the gamma, dwarf and relic constraints. This supports the existence of a fitted sky component under that analysis, not its dark-matter origin or compatibility with the LZ profile.
* A relevant counter-screen, [Wang and Duan, arXiv:2512.12176 (December 13, 2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12176), calculates the antiproton flux for Totani's `b bbar` interpretation and reports it exceeds AMS-02 near 100 GeV. Their background treatment suggests a rate below `2e-26 cm^3/s`, which they conclude makes that specific DM interpretation unviable. This is a published preprint result and needs independent propagation/background review; it should nevertheless be included as a serious conditional constraint.
* The supplied APS paper, [List et al., Phys. Rev. Lett. 136, 231001 (2026), DOI 10.1103/dkcq-6y4f](https://journals.aps.org/prl/abstract/10.1103/dkcq-6y4f), with its open preprint [arXiv:2507.17804](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.17804), is about the Galactic Center Excess (GCE), not the high-latitude 20 GeV halo excess or the cluster line. Its spatial-plus-energy inference finds that, under its best-fit background, the GCE is essentially consistent with Poisson/diffuse emission as expected for DM; a point-source explanation would require a median `O(10^5)` sources and more than 35,000 at 90% confidence, with background systematics able to lower that requirement by roughly an order of magnitude. This supports a diffuse-emission morphology compatible with DM while retaining an astrophysical-source alternative. It does not establish annihilation or identify a common particle.

## Compatibility consequences

The LZ IDM profile is at `m_H=1080 GeV` and its high recoil is inelastic `H+Xe -> A+Xe` scattering. The public nearby H.E.S.S. scan point is `1178.9 GeV`, has a neutral splitting about 1,381 times larger than LZ's, and gives mostly `WW/ZZ` final states. Neither scan row nor the supplied gamma papers is an exact calculation at the LZ profile. In particular:

1. A `b bbar` explanation at 0.5–0.8 TeV cannot be assigned to the 1.08 TeV IDM point by matching mass scale alone. The IDM branching fractions, velocity-dependent annihilation, relic abundance, and detector-folded spectra must be calculated at one frozen parameter point.
2. In a co-traced multicomponent halo, an annihilating subcomponent of fraction `f` has intensity proportional to `f^2 <sigma v>`. Thus a component occupying 10% of the local density would need 100 times the all-DM-fit cross section to preserve the same signal, absent segregation or a different velocity/spatial distribution. A modest ultralight-star fraction cannot by itself rescue a wrong gamma spectrum or cross section.
3. The cluster line, high-latitude continuum, and GCE are separate data products. They may motivate independent replication and public-data analysis, but they do not currently provide a common cross-section target for the IDM-plus-ultralight-star hypothesis.
4. None measures the proposed Casimir-DP residual, and none supplies the microscopic coupling needed to connect an astronomical annihilation signal to laboratory coherence loss.

## Recommended next gate

Keep the gamma excesses as stress tests, not as priors that force a shared bosonic interpretation. First reproduce the exact LZ IDM relic point and its present-day channel-resolved `sigma v` and spectra. Then evaluate those spectra against the released H.E.S.S. and Fermi products, while carrying the antiproton result and dwarf limits as explicit constraints. For any two-component variant, scan the local and Galactic-center fractions separately and apply the `f^2` scaling only when co-tracing is justified. Only a candidate surviving these gates should be passed to the xenon likelihood and the separate Casimir-DP interaction calculation. The measured-overlap objective remains, but no parameter should be tuned to force it.

The PRL GCE result and the cluster-line result are useful methodological leads for source-versus-diffuse discrimination and cross-target tests, respectively. Neither is a detector calibration. Preserve the distinction between a statistically selected excess, a spectrum fitted by an annihilation template, and evidence identifying annihilating dark matter.
