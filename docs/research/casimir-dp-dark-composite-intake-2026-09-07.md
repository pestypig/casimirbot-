# Dark composite scattering: intake and scale screen

Program gate: S1. Exploratory lead; no admitted joint prediction or experimental sensitivity claim.

The user prioritizes measurable signals in both experiments. Composite dark matter is a distinct lead because constituent charge can enhance dark-visible scattering without increasing the elementary visible coupling by the same factor.

## Primary evidence

[Coskuner et al., 1812.07573](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.07573) study asymmetric-dark-matter bound states: extended objects and light mediators reduce high-threshold recoil sensitivity while low-threshold targets can benefit from constituent coherence. Formation and self-interactions also constrain them. Only the abstract was authenticated here; no numerical radius relation or allowed point is imported.

[Monteiro et al., 2007.12067v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12067v2) report a levitated-sensor search for long-range composite-dark-matter impulses. It is a relevant external constraint, not a coherence measurement. Its neutron coupling convention must be translated before applying numerical limits to baryon-normalized pilots.

## Conditional analytic screen

For N identical constituents with additive charge g_d and normalized elastic form factor F_D(0)=1, the Born amplitude contains N g_d F_D(q). At fixed mean bound constituent mass m_bar, M=N m_bar. Fixed dark mass density then implies number flux proportional to 1/N. Thus coupling-squared times flux scales as N |F_D(q)|^2, not N^2, with remaining kinematic factors fixed. Reduced mass, thresholds and strong scattering can change the full rate. No nugget mass-radius relation is assumed.

A representative xenon nuclear mass 122 GeV and recoil 248 keV give q=sqrt(2 m_A E_R)=0.246 GeV, and hbar c/q approximately 0.802 fm. R_D much smaller than this is sufficient for a pointlike elastic response. In comparison hbar/separation for the frozen 250 nm branch separation is approximately 0.789 eV/c. These are diagnostic scales, not response integrals. An extended dark object may retain soft-scattering coherence while losing coherent elastic enhancement at xenon recoil momenta. Breakup, excitation and constituent-resolved scattering need additional structure functions; elastic suppression is not a bound on their total rate.

The existing incident-flux screen gives D <= 4.3431261e-5 for 1 TeV at 776 km/s when branch-dependent impact support is confined to the frozen sphere. Increasing mass reduces that ceiling at fixed density and speed. Long-range tails evade this compact-support premise but require full phase and transport calculations. This is not a general composite exclusion.

## Next calculation and rejection criteria

Authenticate a published microscopic mass-radius-charge relation and elastic/inelastic responses. Fold one parameter set through xenon and finite-sphere coherence, including number flux. Translate neutron versus baryon charge. Check ordinary forces, levitated impulses, self-interactions, formation and overburden before claiming overlap. Use full phase treatment where Born scattering fails.

Reject enhancements that add constituents while silently fixing total mass and flux, extrapolate pointlike xenon scattering outside its regime, or identify an impulse threshold with coherence sensitivity. Homogeneous decoherence still cancels from the unchanged-history boundary ratio: primary visibility loss and a boundary-dependent residual remain separate predictions. No measurable overlap is established.
