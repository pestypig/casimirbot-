# Does a single light mediator rescue the electronic rate ratio?

Date: 2026-09-07. Conditional five-point response scan; not a universal exclusion.

Replace the contact density potential with W(q)=Wref (qref^2+m_A^2)/(q^2+m_A^2), where qref=1 keV and Wref=1 GeV^-2 for calibration. Use the same potential in both the diamond ELF calculation and the atomic xenon spectrum folded through XENON1T response. The normalization Wref cancels from their ratio. This is a new single-density Yukawa hypothesis, not the previously derived second-order virtual interaction and not a nuclear xenon fit.

Retain the shared unattenuated mono-speed population, frozen apparatus, covered material grids, published 186 eV cutoff and fixed ROI from the contact cross-check. At the nominal reference allowance of 24.8 xenon ROI events:

| Mediator mass | Covered diamond D, with local-field effects |
| --- | ---: |
| 1 eV | 4.00082e-19 |
| 1 keV | 3.86910e-19 |
| 10 keV | 7.96924e-20 |
| 100 keV | 1.55793e-22 |
| 1 MeV | 2.73898e-23 |

The lightest sample improves the contact ratio by about 1.53e4, but still misses the theoretical DP comparator by about 7.4e16. This establishes that the particular single-Yukawa momentum dependence does not make the covered electron channel a promising bridge at these sampled masses. It is not a proof for every continuous mass or arbitrary momentum-dependent interaction.

Both folds retain their authenticated response inputs and numerical refinement checks. The scripts save the intermediate spectra, diamond coefficients and final ratios with hashes. The no-local-field comparison is also recorded. It is not a full material uncertainty band. Reference count 24.8 is not a new model-specific statistical limit; the published response and ROI provenance are in the preceding contact cross-check.

The physical reason for improvement is the greater weighting of small momentum transfers in diamond. The numerical result also shows the improvement is insufficient here. Missing subgap excitations and multi-constituent density correlations remain outside this comparison. Attenuation or a different incident population would require a transport calculation; they cannot be assumed to repair the mismatch.

Decision: demote both the constant and this single-Yukawa covered electron-density route under the shared-flux assumptions. Further work should focus on a specified subgap/multi-density mechanism with an actual material response, or a quantitatively supported population difference. Do not repeat these folds with a merely rescaled coupling: the rate ratio is invariant under that change. No measurable shared model has been established.
