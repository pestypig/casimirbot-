# CASPAR source timing: historical hypothesis rejected

Program gate: S1. Primary-source timing screen; no general accelerator-model exclusion.

The preceding thermal/two-step packet established only kinematic permission for an alpha-projectile source. Its possible connection to CASPAR at SURF now fails a basic historical check.

| Date | Authenticated evidence |
|---|---|
| May 17, 2021 | [SURF disassembly announcement](https://sanfordlab.org/news/caspar-collaboration-disassembles-accelerator) reports spring mothballing, accelerator disassembly and storage. |
| June 16, 2023, 21:22:39 UTC | [LZ event timestamp](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.02823v1), Data Analysis. |
| June 20, 2023 | [SURF facility overview](https://indico.sanfordlab.org/event/53/contributions/884/) states that CASPAR's first operating phase was complete and its next phase was planned for 2024. |
| November 25, 2025 | [SURF restart announcement](https://sanfordlab.org/news/caspars-first-beam-particle-accelerator-restart-surf-opens-new-chapter-discovery) reports first beam after roughly four years of hibernation. |

Together these primary facility records support rejecting a contemporaneous CASPAR-beam origin for the June 2023 event. The 2023 overview is close in time to the event, and the later restart account corroborates the interruption. This is stronger evidence than a theory paper listing a facility's accelerator specifications. A minute-resolved beam log was not retrieved, but there is no evidence here of operating beam during the documented shutdown.

## Consequence for the shared prediction program

Close this particular historical source hypothesis. Do not spend a production/geometry simulation on a contemporaneous CASPAR source for this event unless new authenticated evidence contradicts the operating history. The illustrative alpha endpoint remains mathematically valid but supplies no event attribution.

The generic proposal to deliberately accelerate captured dark matter remains a possible future experimental test. It would require an operating source, measured geometry, captured-density and transport calculations, and both detector responses. That prospective experiment does not explain the historical LZ event and cannot substitute for the user's requested shared model.

An assertion that an older beam produced a reservoir persisting until 2023 would be a different source/storage mechanism, requiring a specified lifetime, energy-retention process and delayed release. No such mechanism or population is introduced to evade the timing result. No alternative local accelerator operation is assumed.

Next priority is a physically specified naturally sustained incident population or interaction, subject to the existing spectrum, coherence and external-constraint requirements. The overall goal remains active and unmet.
