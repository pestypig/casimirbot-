## Two different observables

The canonical article, equation (29), defines R4=C_active,sep C_reference,compact/(C_active,compact C_reference,sep). Its primary contraction statistic is separate. The same article's leading-design table specifies a 250 nm tangential split and a common 10 micrometer surface gap. These facts prevent interpreting every single-history scattering exponent as a boundary signal.

Write C_ab=E_ab exp(-D_b+i phi_b), where a labels boundary state, b labels separated/compact history, and E_ab contains other apparatus effects. If the scalar halo and branch histories are unchanged by a, the scalar multiplier cancels exactly from R4. E_ab need not factorize; the cancellation means the scalar does not change its cross-ratio, not that the full measured R4 must equal one. With unequal histories, compute each exponent instead of asserting cancellation. A primary-contraction test can remain sensitive to scalar scattering despite this boundary null.

## Direct plate field

For a linear Yukawa pair potential alpha exp(-ms r)/r, integrating a uniform planar slab of number density n and thickness t gives the potential per test charge

V(z)=2 pi alpha n exp(-ms z) [1-exp(-ms t)]/ms²

in natural units, up to the overall attractive/repulsive sign. This follows by integrating transverse radius first and then source depth. It depends on normal distance only. Two tangential branches at equal height have identical deterministic phase from this ideal slab. A static real potential does not by itself create stochastic coherence loss. Phase fluctuations or a modified incident particle distribution require an additional measured or derived response.

The actual 80 micrometer square plate is finite. Reflection-related paths about its center still have equal potential for a reflection-symmetric density; arbitrary off-center paths do not. The as-built positions, material, thickness, density and covariance are not supplied by this calculation. No finite-plate zero, total-force bound or hardware capability is claimed.

The current leading nucleon-density scalar model has no specified boundary-state-dependent density or scalar susceptibility. Changing electromagnetic impedance is not automatically a change in scalar charge. Binding/electron contributions, temperature changes, source motion, fluctuation spectra and halo transport could break the simplifying assumptions, but must be matched explicitly. They cannot be supplied by assigning the scalar the electromagnetic Casimir response.

## Range screen

At the frozen nearest surface gap g=10 micrometers, the Yukawa exponential factor is exp(-g ms/(hbar c)). It is 9.79772e-23 for 1 eV, 0.00629669 for 0.1 eV and 0.602436 for 0.01 eV. A range equal to the gap requires ms=0.0197327 eV. This is a geometric screening scale, not an exclusion, force magnitude, decoherence bound or permission to retune the pilot. Sphere-center distances exceed the stated surface gap; using g here understates attenuation for the center. Coupling and source prefactors still matter.

An explicit new long-range candidate below roughly 0.02 eV could avoid this particular exponential suppression. It would still need a scalar boundary-state transfer mechanism, finite geometry and appropriate long-range force/stellar constraints. It must be declared a new candidate, not substituted into the frozen 1 eV calculation after observing its failure. Merely extending the range does not remove the tangential symmetry or four-cell cancellation.
