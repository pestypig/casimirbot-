# Explorations of the potential spin–gravity dimensionless response

## Ytterbium-171 energy–spin gravitational clock experiment

**Status:** experimental proposal and analytic design calculation; no apparatus run or measured result is claimed.  
**Theory-graph scope:** independent clock-correlation branch. This proposal does not use the Casimir–DP apparatus or constitute evidence for Diósi–Penrose collapse.

## Research question

Does one dimensionless gravitational response describe (1) an ordinary optical-clock comparison and (2) two distributed entangled-clock preparations in which the association between optical excitation and nuclear spin is reversed?

The motivating question concerned internal correlations during interference of a spatially superposed atom. This experiment tests a related, narrower question: the atoms stay at separate stations while *the location of one optical excitation* is coherently superposed. It does not test center-of-mass interference of one atom across the building.

## Abstract

Use a building-scale gravitational-potential difference to accumulate an optical-clock phase. Measure that baseline conventionally and through two opposite energy–spin correlations. Normalize the results with independent spectroscopy and geometry, then ask whether one dimensionless gravitational response describes all three measurements. The ordinary clock comparison establishes the gravitational rate difference; the quantum mode asks whether reversing the energy–spin association changes the inferred response. The primary outputs are the ordinary response `kappa_cl`, the mean quantum response `kappa_0`, and the correlation-dependent half-difference `eta_ES`. Standard proper-time evolution predicts `(1, 1, 0)` respectively, within uncertainty. These are operational consistency coefficients, not new universal constants.

## Frozen analytic benchmark, not an apparatus freeze

For a stationary weak-field illustration, use the BIPM recommended `nu_0 = 518295836590863.632 Hz`, `g = 9.80665 m s^-2`, and `c = 299792458 m s^-1`:

\[
\Delta U \simeq g\Delta z,\qquad
\frac{\Delta\nu_{\rm GR}}{\nu_0}=\frac{\Delta U}{c^2},\qquad
x_{\rm GR}(T)=2\pi\nu_0\frac{\Delta U}{c^2}T.
\]

At 1 cm separation over 10 s the predicted phase is only 0.03553 rad. A 1% response determination at that height corresponds to about 5.66 microhertz of differential-frequency bias. Raising the baseline makes the gravitational signal larger while the atoms remain accessible in stationary laboratories. Each clock behaves normally locally; the comparison reveals their difference in accumulated proper time.

## Two acquisition modes

1. **Conventional baseline.** Compare unentangled optical clocks with a stabilized frequency-transfer link. Measure and correct link phase, local clock shifts, and trajectory/reference-frame effects. The observable is the corrected differential frequency.
2. **Distributed quantum mode.** Prepare one optical excitation shared coherently between atoms at the upper (`U`) and lower (`L`) station. The photon-mediated entanglement link and the stable phase-reference link have different jobs; a stable frequency-transfer link alone does not establish entanglement.

In an ideal spin-degenerate notation, the two proposed preparations are

\[
|\Psi_+\rangle=\bigl(|e,\uparrow\rangle_U|g,\downarrow\rangle_L+
|g,\downarrow\rangle_U|e,\uparrow\rangle_L\bigr)/\sqrt2,
\]
\[
|\Psi_-\rangle=\bigl(|e,\downarrow\rangle_U|g,\uparrow\rangle_L+
|g,\uparrow\rangle_U|e,\downarrow\rangle_L\bigr)/\sqrt2.
\]

Both have the same local energy and spin populations. What changes is the association of spin with the excitation. Real Zeeman, lattice-light, and other state-dependent shifts break ideal degeneracy. Measure each encoding's actual spectroscopic gap `Delta E_s` and the differential environmental shifts rather than silently treating the gaps as equal. A global spin reversal is a control symmetry in the ideal model; a nonzero difference is not automatically gravitational.

Local calibrated analysis pulses must convert relative phase into a joint parity or equivalent correlation observable. Counting only which atom is excited cannot recover that phase. Multiple analysis phases and interrogation times are needed to unwrap complete cycles, especially at 100 m and 1 s. Population and complementary-basis correlation measurements must establish the intended Bell coherence; a preparation command or one parity fringe is insufficient evidence by itself.

## Estimands and inference

After subtracting calibrated non-gravitational phase, define for each encoding

\[
\kappa_s=\frac{x^{\rm grav,measured}_s}
{(\Delta E_s/\hbar)(\Delta U/c^2)T},\qquad
\kappa_0=\frac{\kappa_++\kappa_-}{2},\qquad
\eta_{ES}=\frac{\kappa_+-\kappa_-}{2}.
\]

The conventional mode gives

\[
\kappa_{\rm cl}=\frac{c^2\Delta\nu_{\rm cl}^{\rm corrected}}
{\nu_0\Delta U}.
\]

The preregistered standard-model target is `kappa_cl = 1`, `kappa_0 = 1`, and `eta_ES = 0`. Report all three with their covariance and absolute uncertainty. A ratio of quantum to conventional responses may be useful, but can hide a deviation common to both.

For identifiability, fit phase against independently measured potential difference and duration, with encoding-dependent offset and differential-frequency nuisance terms. At one fixed height, an unknown frequency bias and the gravitational term both grow linearly with interrogation time, so a time scan alone cannot separate them. A candidate schedule uses approximately 10, 50, and 100 m baselines, several interrogation times, interleaved `+`/`−` preparations, field reversals where valid, phase-reference reversals, and local clock-shift calibrations. Height-dependent magnetic, thermal, trapping, and link effects still require independent measurement; merely changing height does not remove them.

As a **projection-noise-only** illustration at 100 m, 1 s, contrast `C = 0.8`, and `N` *successful* pair measurements split evenly between the encodings,

\[
\sigma(\kappa_0)\simeq\sigma(\eta_{ES})\simeq
\frac{1}{C x_{\rm GR}\sqrt N}.
\]

This gives roughly 1,240, 124,000, and 12.4 million successful pairs for coefficient uncertainties of `1e-3`, `1e-4`, and `1e-5`. Contrast 0.8 is a design assumption for the integrated protocol. Link attempts, losses, dead time, phase tracking, state verification, calibration, and systematic covariance are additional requirements. No calendar duration or achieved sensitivity follows from these counts.

## Hypotheses and evidence gates

- **H0 (standard response):** all three corrected coefficients agree with `(1, 1, 0)` within a preregistered joint uncertainty model.
- **H1 (ordinary differential background):** an apparent encoding difference is explained by spectroscopy, Zeeman/trap shifts, transfer-link phase, preparation/readout bias, or a height-dependent nuisance.
- **H2 (unexplained residual):** a reproducible nonzero `eta_ES` survives controls and independent replication. This would identify an unexplained encoding-dependent response, not establish a new gravitational mechanism by itself.

Before measured-data interpretation, require: independent potential survey; traceable spectroscopy for both encodings; conventional clock comparison; Bell-coherence witness in complementary bases; link and local-field calibrations; phase-unwrapping checks; a locked analysis and uncertainty model; blinded or held-out confirmatory data; and replication of the inference from retained raw counts. A no-go result is recorded if the integrated link cannot preserve and verify sufficient coherence, the gravitational slope is not identifiable against calibrated backgrounds, or uncertainty exceeds the preregistered target.

## Relationship to demonstrated work and claim boundary

The Tokyo Skytree experiment compared transportable *strontium* lattice clocks across about 450 m and tested gravitational redshift at relative uncertainty `9.1e-5`; it was not an entangled-clock test. A separate experiment demonstrated a remote entangled optical-clock network using *strontium ions* over a laboratory-scale link. Published ytterbium-171 control work motivates the four-state optical/nuclear-spin encoding. These are component precedents, not a demonstration of the complete building-scale ytterbium experiment. The quantum equivalence-principle framework motivates the internal-energy question, but does not supply this proposal's measured outcome.

This branch has no direct observable bridge to the Casimir–DP mass-density collapse campaign. Its white-paper format mirrors that campaign's practice of separating frozen theory inputs, acquisition products, competing hypotheses, and permitted claims. It does not inherit any Casimir–DP receipt, certificate, or experimental status.
