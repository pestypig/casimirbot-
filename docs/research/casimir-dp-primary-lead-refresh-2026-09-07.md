## Axion-portal lead: source facts

[Unwin, arXiv:2609.04186v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04186v1) proposes a pseudoscalar-pseudoscalar elastic interaction, realized through an up-quark portal. Its example uses 400 GeV dark matter, a 1 GeV pseudoscalar, gchi = 1.36 and gu = 5.6e-5, with a heavy vector-like quark. Nucleon matching is momentum-dependent and includes a pion pole. The paper gives an indicative event normalization and efficiency-weighted normalized spectra, while acknowledging that a full LZ likelihood is absent. A radial scalar/Higgs interaction is also present and requires a sufficiently small mixed quartic. Its source-supplied significance discussion is based on comparisons with other templates, not a direct likelihood calculation for the complete model.

## Independent assessment and low-momentum calculation

This lead is distinct from the previous even scalar mass coupling. Its leading nonrelativistic channel is O6, involving spin projections along momentum transfer. It must not inherit L10's transverse-spin response or the scalar portal's coherent mass-density source. In an unpolarized spin-zero nucleus, the leading one-body spin response vanishes; the dominant C12 isotope therefore cannot be assigned a macroscopic scalar charge for this channel. C13, defects, electronic effects and scalar/loop companions require separate treatment.

For the leading pole approximation, isolate the momentum factor

    P(q) = q^4 / [(q^2+m_pi^2)^2 (q^2+ma^2)^2].

At ma = 1 GeV and m_pi = 0.13498 GeV, compare P with its value at 246 MeV. The ratio is 1.49e-33 at q = hbar c/R = 0.714 eV and 2.23e-33 at q = hbar c/d = 0.789 eV. It is still only 5.74e-21 at 1 keV. These are operator-shape ratios, not rates: nuclear responses, phase space, exposure, mediator matching and halo weights have not been divided out consistently between targets.

The pion pole does not cancel the q^4 suppression as q approaches zero, because both mediator denominators remain finite there. It can reshape the xenon energy spectrum without automatically producing an appreciable soft local effect. Hard local collisions and induced scalar channels are not bounded by these soft ratios. This makes the proposal a useful independent candidate for a small local signal, not evidence of DP-sized coherence loss.

A spectrum lying between two other unit-normalized spectra does not mathematically bound its likelihood significance between theirs. Nuisance profiling, backgrounds, event coordinates and absolute normalization matter. We will therefore not import the paper's template-based significance bracket as a measured or independently verified confidence statement.

## Seasonal inelastic lead: source facts

[Seasonal dark matter, arXiv:2609.04181v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04181v1) studies a high-splitting inelastic interpretation and predicts a strongly time-dependent signal near the halo endpoint. It uses an approximate reconstructed-energy response and a normalization-profiled shape analysis, with cautions about inference from one event. Its preferred TeV-scale splitting region is larger than in several early examples. The annual response is therefore a useful discriminant, not a measured modulation or an authenticated absolute cross-section.

## Independent target check and methodological limit

For a free stationary target, the largest accessible endothermic splitting is delta_max = reduced_mass v_max^2/2. At a stated 810 km/s endpoint and 1 TeV dark matter, C12 and C13 give approximately 40.35 and 43.67 keV, while the A=131 xenon benchmark gives 396.96 keV. Thus a 350–400 keV splitting cannot be directly supplied by free carbon recoils even when xenon is accessible. Bound-solid energy sharing, pre-existing excited states and loop-induced elastic interactions would need explicit additional calculations; the free-target result is not an all-solid exclusion.

Profiling an overall rate normalization can yield a shape likelihood but cannot provide the absolute normalization needed for our shared local/xenon rate prediction. One event also cannot establish seasonal modulation. Neither a theoretical fit nor a high-tail preference changes the underlying LZ event's unresolved status.
