## Resolution pursuit, not simulation completion

The objective is not to accumulate increasingly elaborate simulations. The
objective is to resolve a bounded scientific question by using simulation,
analytic work, calibration, intervention, and measurement in their proper
evidence roles.

Simulation is an instrument in this program. It may:

- verify equations, units, limits, conservation, constraints, and convergence;
- generate a forward prediction from a frozen source to a registered
  observable;
- determine whether two proposed mechanisms are distinguishable at available
  sensitivity;
- expose parameter degeneracies and design null/control experiments; and
- falsify a candidate that fails its preregistered mathematical or observable
  duties.

Simulation alone may not establish that nature realizes a simulated source,
that an apparatus residual is gravitational, that a boson star exists
astrophysically, or that a quantum-information diagnostic changes spacetime
connectivity.

The resolution pursuit follows this causal skeleton:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\text{microscopic law, environment, and preparation}\\
&\quad\longrightarrow \text{declared classical/quantum state}\\
&\quad\longrightarrow
\left\{
\langle T_{\mu\nu}\rangle,\,
N_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}
\right\}\\
&\quad\longrightarrow
\text{support and instability spectrum}\\
&\quad\longrightarrow
\text{geometry and nonlinear dynamics}\\
&\quad\longrightarrow
\text{observer/detector forward model}\\
&\quad\longrightarrow
\text{measured residual and bounded conclusion}.
\end{aligned}
}
\]

This graph carries four separate conditional questions:

1. **Gravitational sourcing:** what mean stress-energy and stress fluctuations
   does the declared state supply?
2. **Dynamical accessibility:** do density, scale, boundaries, and available
   support permit growth, relaxation, condensation, dispersion, or collapse?
3. **Quantum-state accessibility:** has a relevant coherent state or
   distinguishable mass-density superposition actually been prepared and
   preserved?
4. **Observable identifiability:** can the predicted signature be separated
   from ordinary physics, nuisance parameters, and competing hypotheses?

Accordingly,

\[
\text{can gravitate}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{is unstable}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{is in a prepared mass superposition}
\not\Rightarrow
\text{undergoes gravitational state reduction}.
\]

Each implication becomes an empirical or mathematical question only after its
additional conditions are registered. This preserves the cross-scale insight
that gravitational relevance is universal while collapse, condensation,
coherent superposition, and quantum state reduction are conditionally
accessible regimes with different rate laws.

### Causal marginalization contract

The campaign must marginalize uncertain nuisance causes, not erase the physical
mediators that make a source-to-observable claim testable. For hypothesis
\(H_a\), data \(D\), and registered nuisance vector \(\theta_a\),

\[
P(D\mid H_a)
=
\int
P(D\mid H_a,\theta_a)\,
P(\theta_a\mid H_a)\,
d\theta_a .
\]

The hypothesis set must be frozen before held-out comparison and must include
the strongest applicable ordinary alternatives. Depending on the lane, these
may include thermal, mechanical, electromagnetic, material, environmental
decoherence, numerical/discretization, conventional GR, boundary-QED,
registered collapse-model, and unknown-residual hypotheses.

The mediating chain remains explicit:

\[
\text{controlled source intervention}
\rightarrow
\text{tensor/state change}
\rightarrow
\text{predicted dynamics with causal timing}
\rightarrow
\text{detector response}.
\]

A mechanism gains support only when its amplitude, phase, spatial dependence,
parameter scaling, causal delay, and null/control behavior survive the
registered marginalization and held-out test. A residual at one expected
frequency or a successful simulation is insufficient.

### Resolution outcomes

The pursuit is allowed to resolve negatively or inconclusively. Its terminal
outcome grammar is:

| Outcome | Meaning | Maximum claim |
|---|---|---|
| **known physics recovered** | Calibrations and held-out data agree with registered ordinary models | The apparatus/pipeline recovered the tested regime |
| **candidate falsified** | A frozen candidate fails a hard mathematical, causal, or observable duty | The tested candidate is rejected in the registered domain |
| **bounded exclusion** | Sensitivity and power exclude a registered parameter region after nuisance marginalization | Only the preregistered region is excluded |
| **models not identifiable** | Competing models remain observationally degenerate at achieved sensitivity | No mechanism preference |
| **unexplained residual** | A residual survives registered controls but lacks unique mechanism discrimination | An anomaly remains; no gravity or collapse identification |
| **model favored** | One registered model wins the held-out comparison with required robustness and independent replay | Conditional model preference, not universal proof |
| **source-to-observable closure** | One independently specified source closes tensor, dynamics, geometry, observable, and replay duties | Closure only for the authenticated model and domain |

Positive discovery is therefore not the only successful resolution. A
well-powered null, a bounded exclusion, an identified degeneracy, or an
authenticated falsification advances the program by removing causal
possibilities without inventing a replacement.

## Phase-regime and topological-defect calibration

A Bose--Einstein condensate is a distinct quantum state or phase of matter:
below the applicable transition regime, a macroscopic occupation develops in
one quantum mode and the system acquires a collective order parameter,

\[
\Psi(\mathbf x)=\sqrt{n(\mathbf x)}e^{i\theta(\mathbf x)}.
\]

In a finite trapped gas, the plan must state whether it is using strict
thermodynamic-limit phase language, a finite-size crossover, or an operational
laboratory classifier. Cooling alone is not the definition. Condensate
occupation, coherence, symmetry/order, response, and uncertainty must support
the label.

The phase lane extends the shared causal interface:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\text{microscopic Hamiltonian/effective action and controls}\\
&\quad\longrightarrow \text{order parameters and phase regime}\\
&\quad\longrightarrow \text{effective current, stress, and fluctuations}\\
&\quad\longrightarrow \text{support and excitation spectrum}\\
&\quad\longrightarrow \text{defects and driven dynamics}\\
&\quad\longrightarrow \text{observable and phase classification}.
\end{aligned}
}
\]

Applicable controls include temperature \(T\), atom number/density, contact
scattering length \(a_s\), dipolar length \(a_{dd}\), trap geometry, magnetic
field, quench protocol, and rotation rate \(\Omega\). The classification must
not rely on one visual density image.

| Regime | Required order/response evidence | Prohibited shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| Thermal or noncondensed gas | No authenticated macroscopic condensate occupation under the chosen finite-size classifier | Inferring coherence from low temperature alone |
| BEC | Condensate occupation and first-order coherence/order-parameter evidence | Calling occupation a mass-branch superposition |
| Superfluid BEC | BEC evidence plus phase stiffness or registered superfluid response | Treating BEC and superfluidity as universally identical definitions |
| Supersolid | Coherent/superfluid response plus persistent density/crystalline order in the same state | Calling any separated droplet array supersolid |
| Incoherent droplet crystal | Density order with negligible or failed global phase-locking/superfluid link under the registered classifier | Promoting spatial modulation alone to supersolidity |
| Vortex-bearing phase | Parent-phase evidence plus quantized circulation/winding and defect-resolved dynamics | Calling a vortex a new bulk phase by itself |

Candidate observables include:

Here \(f_0\) is a declared condensate fraction, \(g^{(1)}\) is first-order

### Phase changes are not one kind of collapse

The graph and papers must distinguish:

- a thermal-to-BEC condensation transition or finite-size crossover;
- a superfluid-to-supersolid transition with additional spatial order;
- loss of global coherence into an incoherent droplet-crystal regime;
- soft-mode or roton-driven instability;
- vortex nucleation as entry into a topological-defect sector;
- driven synchronization following angular-momentum transfer;
- gravitational Jeans/EKG instability;
- environmental decoherence; and
- a registered objective state-reduction hypothesis.

These processes may interact, but none is evidence for another without a
typed, tested bridge. In particular, vortex nucleation is ordinarily a
topological excitation threshold within a parent phase, and driven
synchronization is a dynamical diagnostic rather than automatically an
equilibrium thermodynamic phase boundary.

The 2024 observation of quantized vortices in a dipolar \(^{164}\mathrm{Dy}\)
supersolid supplies a laboratory benchmark for simultaneous density order,
phase-coherent hydrodynamics, and defect dynamics. The 2025 rotating-supersolid
experiment further shows a delayed superfluid response: above a critical drive,
vortex nucleation seeds synchronization of the supersolid with the rotating
field. These results motivate a causal calibration chain,

\[
\text{rotational intervention}
\rightarrow
\text{vortex nucleation}
\rightarrow
\text{angular-momentum redistribution}
\rightarrow
\text{synchronization observable}.
\]

They do not establish appreciable laboratory self-gravity, a boson star, a
macroscopic mass-density branch superposition, gravitational state reduction,
or NHM2 source closure.

## Matter, curvature, and surface language

A boson star is not curvature without matter. In the standard model it is a
regular configuration of a massive complex scalar field coupled to gravity.
Its gravitational field is sourced by the field's full stress-energy tensor,
including time-derivative, spatial-gradient, mass-potential, and any
self-interaction contributions. A particle interpretation may associate the
field with massive spin-0 quanta and a conserved charge, but the classical EKG
solution must not be described as a direct particle count unless the quantum
state and normalization have been supplied.

The papers and graph should enforce these wording corrections:

- Bose statistics permit unbounded mode occupation in the ideal theory; they
  do not permit an actually infinite number of particles or infinite energy in
  a finite physical system.
- A boson star has no material surface at a unique finite radius. Its scalar
  profile normally decays into an exterior tail, so radii such as \(R_{90}\) or
  \(R_{99}\) must state the enclosed-mass or charge convention.
- Ordinary stars also do not have a hard edge. A photosphere or other optical
  depth convention defines an effective surface.
- A black hole is not usefully defined as "pure curvature located at a
  singularity." Its operational distinction is the event horizon and exterior
  conserved charges; the classical singularity is not a material source that
  can be inserted into the boson-star comparison table.
- "Quantum pressure" is acceptable shorthand only when bound to scalar
  gradient energy or to the Madelung/Schrödinger-Poisson effective stress in
  the nonrelativistic limit. Repulsive self-interaction is a separate support
  term and depends on the potential and sign of the coupling.

The comparative source statement is therefore:

> Material bodies, thermal stars, bosonic condensates, and black holes can all
> produce exterior gravitational fields, but their interior closure,
> constitutive support, surface convention, stability problem, and observable
> response are different.

## Jeans instability and the support-competition bridge

The ordinary Jeans calculation already present in the Casimir-DP Stage 4.2D
lane is a thermal-gas recovery:

\[
\omega^2=c_s^2 k^2-4\pi G\rho_0,
\qquad
\lambda_J=c_s\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{G\rho_0}}.
\]

It tests pressure support against self-gravity in a specified hydrodynamic
regime. It is not a universal gravity-onset, quantum-collapse, or boson-star
formation threshold.

For a nonrelativistic coherent massive scalar described by the
Schrödinger-Poisson or Gross-Pitaevskii-Poisson system, the corresponding
linearized static-background relation has a different constitutive term:

\[
\omega^2=
\frac{\hbar^2 k^4}{4m_b^2}
+c_{\rm int}^2 k^2
-4\pi G\rho_0.
\]

For a free field, \(c_{\rm int}=0\), giving

\[
k_{J,b}=\left(\frac{16\pi G\rho_0m_b^2}{\hbar^2}\right)^{1/4}.
\]

In an expanding background the registered perturbation equation must also
carry Hubble damping and scale-factor dependence:

\[
\ddot\delta+2H\dot\delta+
\left(
\frac{\hbar^2k^4}{4m_b^2a^4}
+\frac{c_{\rm int}^2k^2}{a^2}
-4\pi G\rho
\right)\delta=0.
\]

These equations define a **bosonic Jeans or wave-support crossover**, not a
completed compact object. Crossing it establishes linear growth eligibility.
Formation still requires nonlinear collapse, energy redistribution or
ejection, relaxation, equilibrium identification, and a stability test.

The graph should expose one common parent without erasing the constitutive
differences:

| Descendant system | Inward driver | Registered support/response | Appropriate diagnostic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Potato-radius moon or rubble body | Self-gravity | Yield strength, porosity, friction, rheology | Strength-to-gravity crossover and shape relaxation |
| Molecular cloud | Self-gravity | Thermal, turbulent, rotational, and magnetic support | Jeans and free-fall scales with environmental extensions |
| Normal star | Self-gravity | EOS pressure, radiation pressure, transport, and nuclear heating | Hydrostatic structure and stellar-evolution stability |
| Nonrelativistic bosonic field | Self-gravity | Gradient/wave stress and model-dependent self-interaction | Bosonic Jeans scale and Schrödinger-Poisson evolution |
| Relativistic boson star | Full EKG gravity | Relativistic scalar stress and self-interaction | Radial-mode spectrum, turning points, constraints, and nonlinear evolution |

The potato-radius, thermal Jeans, stellar hydrostatic, and bosonic Jeans rows
therefore share `gravity_versus_support_problem` but must be connected by
`analogous_balance_not_same_constitutive_law`.

Useful formation references include

## Boson-star formation evidence

An equilibrium solution proves existence of a configuration in a model; it
does not prove that cosmological or astrophysical initial conditions form it.
Every proposed formation route must bind a formation-specific chain:

\[
\text{field abundance and initial spectrum}
\rightarrow
\text{linear growth eligibility}
\rightarrow
\text{nonlinear collapse}
\rightarrow
\text{relaxation}
\rightarrow
\text{equilibrium match}
\rightarrow
\text{survival and observable}.
\]

The three frequently discussed routes require separate claim boundaries:

1. **Primordial or early-universe overdensity.** A scalar field, potential,
   production history, background expansion, perturbation spectrum, and
   abundance must be specified. Expansion and cooling do not by themselves
   imply condensation into isolated stable stars.
2. **Wave/fuzzy-dark-matter halo formation.** Schrödinger-Poisson simulations
   can produce self-bound solitonic cores inside fluctuating halos. Such a core
   is not automatically an isolated relativistic boson star, and a galactic
   center is not thereby shown to replace a supermassive black hole.
   "Gravitational cooling" means relaxation through scalar-wave energy
   ejection or redistribution; it should not be reduced to ordinary collisional
   cooling or dynamical friction.
3. **Black-hole superradiance.** A massive bosonic mode can extract rotational
   energy and angular momentum from a spinning black hole and populate a bound
   cloud when the superradiant and confinement conditions hold. The result is
   a cloud around a pre-existing black hole, not a standalone boson star.
   Synchronized hairy-black-hole solutions are a separate nonlinear family and
   cannot be inferred from the existence of a perturbative cloud. See
   [Brito, Cardoso, and Pani](https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.06570).

A formation receipt should report:

- field mass, spin, potential, couplings, and cosmological abundance;
- initial overdensity spectrum, phase/coherence assumptions, and environment;
- thermal, wave, self-interaction, rotation, tidal, and expansion terms;
- mass, energy, and charge flux through the boundary;
- collapse, dispersion, ejection, merger, and horizon diagnostics;
- match distance to a registered equilibrium family;
- stability duration in dynamical and light-crossing units; and
- a predicted observable that distinguishes the route from alternatives.

Formation maturity is distinct from the E0--E7 evidence ladder:

| Formation level | Required result |
|---|---|
| F0 | Field model, abundance, and initial-condition distribution are defined |
| F1 | Linear perturbations enter the registered growing regime |
| F2 | Nonlinear evolution forms a bound overdensity |
| F3 | Energy/charge accounting demonstrates relaxation without hidden dissipation |
| F4 | The remnant matches a registered equilibrium family within frozen tolerances |
| F5 | Perturbations demonstrate survival, migration, dispersion, or collapse under a frozen classification |
| F6 | A route-specific observable survives null-model and selection-effect tests |

No F-level should be inferred from equilibrium existence alone.

## Quantum coherence, entanglement, and EPR boundary

Bosonic occupation, condensate coherence, and quantum entanglement are not
synonyms. Symmetrization or macroscopic occupation of one mode does not by
itself provide an EPR pair, a bipartite entanglement entropy, or evidence that
spacetime connectivity has changed.

The standard EKG boson-star solve is normally a classical or mean-field
calculation. It provides \(\phi\), \(T_{\mu\nu}[\phi]\), geometry, and classical
stability. It does not provide a quantum density matrix or a measured
entanglement receipt. A quantum extension would have to declare subsystems and
state preparation and then compute quantities such as:

\[
S_A=-\operatorname{Tr}(\rho_A\ln\rho_A),
\qquad
I(A:B)=S_A+S_B-S_{AB},
\]

together with connected stress-tensor correlations or a noise kernel when
semiclassical fluctuations matter:

\[
N_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}(x,y)
=\frac12
\left\langle
\left\{\Delta\hat T_{\mu\nu}(x),
\Delta\hat T_{\rho\sigma}(y)\right\}
\right\rangle.
\]

This creates a legitimate interface with the existing ER=EPR work, but only as
one of the following typed relations:

- `shares_quantum_state_observable_with`
- `supplies_stress_correlation_input_to`
- `tests_mean_field_sufficiency_for`
- `provides_non_holographic_control_for`
- `coherence_does_not_establish_entanglement`
- `entanglement_does_not_establish_metric_connectivity`
- `holographic_connectivity_context_only`

The existing ER=EPR Stage-1 contract remains controlling: entanglement/
connectivity reasoning is interpretive in the registered holographic models,
not standalone engineering, local-wormhole, stress-energy, or NHM2 evidence.
A boson-star result can provide a non-holographic gravitating-matter control and
a test bed for state/stress correlations. It cannot promote ER=EPR, CL status,
wormhole inventory, or propulsion authority.

## Shared evidence receipt

Every future cross-system result must bind at least:

1. **Source identity:** field theory, action or constitutive law, boundary and
   initial data, parameters, units, conventions, and exact input hashes.
2. **State identity:** classical, quantum, semiclassical, coherent, thermal, or
   effective state; ensemble and averaging prescription; preparation or
   simulation provenance.
3. **Full tensor:** every required component of \(T_{\mu\nu}\), its ADM
   projections \(E,J_i,S_{ij}\), sign conventions, tetrad or coordinate basis,
   and renormalization prescription when applicable.
4. **Closure residuals:** \(\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu}\), Hamiltonian constraint
   \(\mathcal H\), momentum constraints \(\mathcal M_i\), boundary residuals,
   asymptotic conditions, and registered norms.
5. **Convergence:** frozen grids or resolutions, expected convergence order,
   observed order, roundoff/conditioning evidence, and first-failure rule.
6. **Geometry identity:** formulation, gauge, lapse, shift, spatial metric,
   extrinsic curvature, dynamic/effective classification, and solution
   provenance.
7. **Observable contract:** detector or observer family, forward model,
   calibration, uncertainty budget, nuisance parameters, and predicted
   observable before comparison with held-out data.
8. **Residual classification:** synthetic or measured status, null and control
   outcomes, statistical power, systematic closure, and maximum permitted
   claim.
9. **Falsifier:** a quantitative result that rejects or blocks the registered
   model, approximation, or interpretation.
10. **Independence:** implementation/runtime lineage, data provenance,
    preregistration time, replay status, and any shared dependencies.
11. **Causal discrimination:** frozen hypotheses, explicit mediators,
    intervention and timing contract, nuisance priors, identifiability/power,
    held-out comparison rule, and companion signatures.
12. **Resolution outcome:** the exact terminal outcome from the registered
    grammar, excluded domain when applicable, unresolved alternatives,
    invalidation dependencies, and maximum permitted conclusion.
13. **Phase/defect extension when applicable:** control path, order parameters,
    symmetry/operational classifier, finite-size status, excitation/critical
    evidence, vortex or other defect topology, effective current/stress,
    phase alternatives, and phase-transition receipt reference.

No receipt may silently replace a missing field with a default, infer a full
tensor from scalar energy alone, or call a residual gravitational solely
because it has gravitational dimensions.
