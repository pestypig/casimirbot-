# Conditional virtual-electron leading density contribution

Exploratory component prediction, September 7, 2026. Same candidate coupling as the earlier one-raw-high-window xenon normalization; no parameter refit.

In the leading nonrelativistic electron expansion, m_e*bar(e)*e becomes m_e times electron number density, and O_e^(2)00 becomes 3*m_e/4 times that density. Keeping only those leading terms gives W_density=2*m_e*(c0+3*c2/4), in the previously checked potential convention. With the conditional scalar/spin-2 coefficients this is 1.80307726e-12 GeV^-2. The [operator convention is C1-C2 of Berlin and Kling](https://inspirehep.net/files/8e9cf20ffaddb4d651a261ac379eed74).

This defines a specific leading-density component that can be evaluated even while the full matching remains incomplete. Applying the authenticated unit-contact material response gives D_covered=3.58490147e-43 with local-field effects and 4.60189569e-43 without them. These inherit the response's 0.34-0.36 percent grid-refinement differences, bulk isotropic zero-temperature approximation, frozen hold/separation and mono-speed 100 GeV incident population. The covered domain is 5.5-50 eV excitation energy and momenta through 30.614 keV/c.

The two material values are comparison calculations, not a full uncertainty interval. This component omits kinetic/stress corrections, spin response, subgap/surface channels and interactions involving different constituents. The leading reduction does not bound those terms. Complete Majorana diagram accounting and nonforward matching remain unresolved, so this is a conditional leading-component prediction, not a final model prediction or experimental limit.

Unlike the earlier rejected instantaneous-electron shortcut, this coefficient comes from the forward full-gap vector-loop work and its scalar/spin-2 extraction. That progress does not authorize promotion beyond the approximation stated here. The same leading coefficient is reconstructed independently from the preceding Ward packet, with agreement better than 1e-10 relative; source hashes and numerical results are stored in the companion JSON.

Decision: the leading electronic density contribution offers no evidence of measurable overlap at this xenon normalization. Do not devote further precision work to this tiny component unless the coefficient changes after a substantive matching correction. The unresolved spin/material channels need their own response rather than arbitrary enhancement of the density calculation. The shared-model goal remains active and unfulfilled.
