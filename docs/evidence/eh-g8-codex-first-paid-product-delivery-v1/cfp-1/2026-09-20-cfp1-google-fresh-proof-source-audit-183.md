| Boundary | Observed fact | CFP-1 consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Google web sign-in | `GoogleSignInButton.tsx` initializes Google Identity Services with `auto_select` and posts a returned credential plus CSRF cookie to `/api/auth/google`. `google-auth.ts` verifies the ID token audience, requires `sub`, then creates a CasimirBot account session. Its payload type/route do not read or require `auth_time`, nonce or an operation-bound intent. | A current successful Google sign-in proves the accepted provider subject for session creation. | Existing Auth0 step-up | `desktop-auth0-step-up.ts` gates installed security management to a developer session and native desktop session. `auth0-step-up.ts` requests `max_age`, validates the returned `auth_time` and MFA-related claims, then uses a bounded receipt. | It is a useful source pattern but not an ordinary `user` Google route or a public P1 enrollment acceptance. Reusing its receipt shape does not silently make the developer endpoint public. |
| Provider contract | [OpenID Connect Core](https://openid.net/specs/openid-connect-core-1_0-18.html) requires `auth_time` when `max_age` or an Essential `auth_time` claim is requested and specifies checking the returned value. [Google's OIDC reference](https://developers.google.com/identity/openid-connect/reference) documents requesting `auth_time` with an Essential ID-token claim and identifies `sub` rather than email as the stable account key. | A separate Google-compatible fresh-auth flow is technically plausible, but these documents alone do not prove this project's Google client/API path can obtain an operation-bound fresh result. The exact flow and live provider response require account/security review and CFP-2 installed tests. Do not infer Google support for an undocumented request parameter from the generic OIDC specification. |

**Proposed D02 proof rule:** retain Google as an intended verified sign-in method, but require a separate first-party, operation-bound fresh-authentication flow before a public Google user can enroll/recover a P1 installation or perform another sensitive change. It must request a signed authentication-time claim through a supported provider flow, verify issuer, audience, expiry, stable subject, state/nonce and the returned `auth_time` against the selected maximum age and intent creation time, and mint only a single-use short receipt bound to account, purpose, device/target and service epoch. A user-selected account, same email, ordinary GIS auto-select token, recent CasimirBot session, provider token `iat`, or possession of an old installation grant alone must not satisfy that rule. If the provider does not return an acceptable `auth_time` in the chosen flow, fail closed with a recoverable alternative that has separately reviewed subject-linkage and fresh-proof semantics. Do not advertise a complete Google P1 journey from the current login button alone.

**Later acceptance fixtures:** GP-01 Google sign-in creates an ordinary session under the same stable `sub`; GP-02 a fresh operation-bound provider result for that `sub` yields one receipt and one installation/scope change; GP-03 missing, stale or future `auth_time` denies; GP-04 wrong `sub`, same email/different `sub`, wrong audience/issuer, mismatched nonce/state or replay denies; GP-05 auto-select/current cookie/old ID token without fresh proof denies; GP-06 provider outage or unsupported auth-time claim leaves an explicit recovery state and no grant, while existing independently valid stop/revoke remains governed by its own authority; GP-07 signed installed P1 wizard completes the Google-only enrollment and same-ID recovery without a manual bearer paste. Capture redacted provider claim shape and receipt/intent revisions; never retain raw ID tokens in public evidence.
