# CFP-1 New York tax advisory-opinion route screen — 2026-09-20

The owner's selected initial seller is an individual in New York City without an LLC. New York's [Form AD-1.8 instructions](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pdf/current_forms/misc/ad1_8i.pdf) permit an individual to petition on their own behalf about a hypothetical or projected future transaction. An opinion binds the Commissioner only for the petitioner and the accurately stated facts. The instructions expressly exclude **nexus** determinations; this route cannot by itself decide registration duties in other states, income tax, NYC business filings, Stripe identity, Minecraft rights or privacy terms. The published instructions and [petition form](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pdf/current_forms/misc/ad1_8_fill_in.pdf) are marked 6/17; verify the current procedure before any filing.

Two [2024](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pubs_and_bulls/advisory_opinions/sales/24-42s.htm) [opinions](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pubs_and_bulls/advisory_opinions/sales/24-8s.htm) illustrate why the exact bundle matters. In their respective facts, the Department treated paid mobile-app/dashboard access and paid web-portal access as taxable prewritten software; a single-price bundle in the former was taxed as a whole. The latter discusses customer-user location when software is used across jurisdictions. Those opinions bind only their petitioners and **do not classify CasimirBot**. The possible New York software tax and the location of both host and guest are specific F-04 questions, not an assumption that the subscription is exempt or taxable.

An eventual product-specific request would need a stable, accurate fact statement for the **separately free local MCP tools** and the **charged hosted room service**, including what software the host and invited guest can access, whether a customer downloads software as part of the paid supply, the one-sponsor price, the seven-day no-card trial, where host and guest use or direct the hosted service, and who is merchant of record. The first paid action and any mixed service/software component must be identified as actually selected. Ask for classification of the single recurring charge, the relevant customer-use/sourcing rule, and the implications of any separately stated item only if the final offer actually has one. Keep nexus, registration timing and non-New-York states in the qualified review's separate scope.

The disclosure tradeoff is material. The [instructions](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pdf/current_forms/misc/ad1_8i.pdf) say issued opinions are published with petitioner name, address and identifiers redacted; additional redactions depend on a specific request and Department judgment. The petition itself may be disclosed under FOIL subject to protections, and the signed [form](https://www.tax.ny.gov/pdf/current_forms/misc/ad1_8_fill_in.pdf) consents to publication. Do not put private financial forecasts, unreleased source details or security design into a draft without reviewing necessity and disclosure. A tax attorney or qualified preparer can assess whether this route is proportionate and formulate complete facts; the Department may reject an insufficient petition. No completion time, fee, favorable outcome or confidentiality promise was established by this screen.

**Decision consequence:** offer this as an optional, public authority route for the New York sales-tax **classification** part of F-04 after D03/D07 and the merchant arrangement are stable enough to describe. It does not replace the qualified product-specific D11 rights, privacy, accounting and multi-jurisdiction review, nor resolve the held game-counsel inquiry. Nothing was submitted or changed. CFP-1 remains active (`specified`), CFP-2/3 blocked and G8 active.
